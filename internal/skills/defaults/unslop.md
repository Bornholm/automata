# Remove the AI tells from a text

Load this before writing anything long meant for someone else — an email, a
document, an announcement — or when the user asks you to clean up a text.
Short conversational replies do not need it.

## Delete these on sight

- Opening filler: "Great question", "Absolutely", "I'd be happy to",
  "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing".
- Closing filler: "In conclusion", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you
  need anything else", "At the end of the day".
- Tired vocabulary: delve, tapestry, testament to, realm, landscape,
  navigate (figurative), leverage, robust, seamless, comprehensive,
  cutting-edge, game-changer, unlock, elevate, foster, myriad.
- The negation-flip: "It's not just X, it's Y". "This isn't about X — it's
  about Y". Say the thing directly instead.
- Rule-of-three padding: "clear, concise, and compelling". Keep the one
  adjective that carries meaning; drop the other two.
- Em-dashes used for rhythm rather than for a genuine aside. One per
  paragraph is plenty; a comma or a full stop is usually better.
- Hedging stacks: "it's worth noting that", "generally speaking",
  "in many cases it could be argued".
- Emoji in section headings, and bold on entire sentences.

## Then rewrite what remains

- One idea per sentence. Break any sentence you cannot read aloud in one
  breath.
- Prefer concrete verbs to abstract nouns: "we decided" over "the
  decision-making process resulted in".
- Cut roughly one word in five. The meaning should survive intact; if it
  does not, put the word back.
- Keep the author's voice when cleaning up someone else's text: fix the
  tics, do not rewrite their personality out.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. Uniform rhythm is itself a tell.

## Check before delivering

Read the first and last sentence. If either could open or close any text on
any subject, rewrite it so it could not.
