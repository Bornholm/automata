# Translate a text

## Decide the register first

Match the source: a message between friends stays informal, a formal letter
stays formal. In French, pick "tu" or "vous" from the source relationship
and never switch mid-text. When the source is ambiguous and the choice
matters, ask — one short question is better than a wrong register
throughout.

## Leave these untouched

- Proper nouns, brand and product names, people's names.
- Code, identifiers, file names, commands, URLs, placeholders like
  `{name}` or `%s`.
- Quoted material whose exact wording matters (a legal clause, a citation):
  translate it, but keep the original alongside when the wording carries
  weight.

## Keep the shape

Preserve line breaks, lists, headings and emphasis exactly. A translation
that reflows the layout forces the reader to re-find their bearings.

Numbers, dates and units: keep the value, adapt the notation to the target
language (decimal separator, date order). Never convert currencies or
measurements unless asked — that is a decision, not a translation.

## Idioms

Translate the intent, not the words. An idiom with no equivalent becomes
plain language rather than a literal rendering nobody says. If the source
plays on words in a way the target cannot carry, render the meaning and say
in one line what was lost.

## Deliver

Give the translation alone, with no commentary, unless you had to make a
judgement call — then add one short line about it at the end. Never explain
your method.
